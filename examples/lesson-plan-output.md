# Example Implementation Plan

> Illustrative prototype output based on the **Visual Conceptual Approach**.

## Context

- Subject: Mathematics
- Grade: Grade 4
- Duration: 40–45 minutes
- Approved pedagogical decision: Visual Conceptual Approach

## Learning objective

Students explain the meaning of equivalence using a visual representation and connect the representation to a symbolic equation before moving to procedural solving.

## Success criterion

A student can explain, in their own words, why both sides remain equal and can represent that explanation with a drawing or visual model without relying only on memorized steps.

## Warm-up

Display a balance with different objects on both sides. Ask: “When does the balance remain equal?” Use responses to activate the concept of equivalence before introducing symbols.

## Lesson sequence

1. Introduce the balance model and connect it to the idea of two equal sides.
2. Translate the model into a simple symbolic equation.
3. In groups, students draw their own balance and write the matching equation.
4. Compare group models and correct misconceptions aloud.
5. Complete a short independent explanation task without immediately solving the equation.

## Differentiation

- Provide color-coded balance templates for students who need support.
- Offer two-sided equations for students ready for additional challenge.
- Provide vocabulary cards for “equivalent,” “side,” and “equal.”

## Assessment

Observe whether students can explain the balance relationship during group work, then review the individual exit response.

## Exit card

Draw a balance representing a simple equation and explain why it remains balanced.
